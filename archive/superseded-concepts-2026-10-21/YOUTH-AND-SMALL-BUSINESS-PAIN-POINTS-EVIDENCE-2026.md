# Where earning breaks: South African youth and small-business evidence

Research cut-off: 11 October 2026. Prepared for FNB App of the Year hackathon concept selection.

## Decision in brief

The most promising combined youth/enterprise opportunity is helping a small business turn a real customer order into completed, paid work, with a supervised paid entry point for a young person where appropriate. Call the concept **OrderBridge** for now.

The central hypothesis is that some businesses have commercially useful work that could be delegated, but lack the time, coordination, trust or cash timing to do so. Public evidence establishes several surrounding constraints. It does **not** establish how often this particular combination occurs or that owners will pay for the proposed solution. Validate that before committing to the full product.

Start with one local clothing or uniform producer and an actual order. Stock replenishment for spazas has stronger recent local interview evidence and is a good alternative if supplier and delivery partners are easier to access.

Do not pitch a universal AI business adviser, another CV generator, or an app that claims to create demand automatically. The evidence points to practical differences between sectors. Digital coordination can address some; household purchasing power, crime, premises costs and insufficient aggregate employment require wider interventions.

## How to interpret this research

- This is desk research into published surveys, reports, interviews and public accounts. No interviews were conducted by us and nobody was contacted.
- Publication date and measurement date are different. Recent publication does not turn older fieldwork into 2026 data.
- National household surveys establish scale. Representative enterprise surveys help compare reported barriers. Platform surveys, convenience samples and individual interviews reveal mechanisms but cannot establish national prevalence.
- A sector is not the same as registration status. Salons, manufacturers, IT firms and retailers may be formal or informal, and may have very different sizes and business models.
- Percentages from different populations, questions and periods cannot be combined into a national ranking. Several questions allow multiple responses.
- Product suggestions below are our inferences, not recommendations or demonstrated effects from the source authors.

## 1. Youth: the barriers between looking and earning

### Scale

Stats SA's Q2 2026 QLFS, released 11 August, reports headline unemployment of 33.6%. Among people aged 15–34, the NEET rate was 45.4%. NEET means not in employment, education or training; it is not the unemployment rate. The QLFS is the appropriate national baseline, but it does not identify a single app-solvable cause. Its revised informality measures also have comparability limitations across earlier periods. [Stats SA, Q2 2026, tables A and 7d](https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0211/P02112ndQuarter2026.pdf)

### Access costs and the experience loop

Youth Capital/JOBJACK's online survey of over 10,000 young respondents, conducted in 2023 and published in February 2024, reported average job-search spending of R1,469 per month, including R700 transport. Respondents requested opportunities near home, trusted application channels and help with applications; work experience and networks were recurring themes. These are self-reported results from an online sample, not a representative estimate of today's costs. [Youth Capital release](https://youthcapital.co.za/job-seekingcosts-2024/)

### Starting is different from sustaining income

Harambee's August 2026 Breaking Barriers report highlights the unsupported transition into earning, care responsibilities, spatial exclusion and transport costs. Its SA Youth CV already captures informal experience. An informal-experience CV by itself is therefore weak differentiation. The report draws on multiple datasets with different periods and populations; its findings should not be presented as one national survey. [Harambee, August 2026](https://www.harambee.co.za/the-ecosystem-effect-why-inclusive-design-creates-inclusive-growth/)

Harambee's November 2025 report contrasts independent self-employment with earning through intermediaries and argues for practical support around livelihoods. Its platform observations are useful for designing a supported earning pathway, but differences between groups do not prove the causal effect of an intermediary. [Harambee, November 2025](https://www.harambee.co.za/fueling-the-engine-of-self-employment-a-viable-path-towards-sustained-livelihoods-for-young-people/)

The February 2026 report also supports demand-led pathways and employer participation. It includes an income survey of 4,508 working participants and a tracer of 753 people previously placed in global business services. Outcomes among selected placed respondents must not be described as the placement rate for all unemployed applicants. [Harambee, February 2026 PDF](https://www.harambee.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/HAR2644-Breaking-Barriers-Feb-2026-R1.pdf)

### Public accounts add texture, not population estimates

A public South African forum account describes an experienced developer struggling to obtain repeat freelance work and receiving little human feedback despite completing assessments. Identity and circumstances are unverified. This is a lead for investigating customer acquisition and hiring feedback, not evidence that all IT businesses face the same problem. [Public discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSouthAfrica/comments/1usha54/youre_underpaid_struggling_financially_and_cant/)

GroundUp's August 2026 reporting includes jobseekers relying on piece work, family support and grants. Their ages are not established in the article, so these accounts should not be labelled a youth sample. The politically charged recruitment setting also makes it important to distinguish reported beliefs from explanations supported by evidence. [GroundUp, 28 August 2026](https://groundup.org.za/article/desperate-jobseekers-pin-their-hopes-on-march-march/)

### Product implications — our synthesis

| Barrier | Practical design response | What remains outside the app |
|---|---|---|
| Paying before earning | Show transport/data costs before acceptance; nearby work; employer-funded access where available | An unfunded app cannot subsidise every interview |
| No credible first experience | Paid supervised tasks with clear acceptance and a verifiable work record | An AI-generated certificate is not employer trust |
| Weak networks and feedback | Named business contact, explicit task status and reasons for rejection | Employers must participate |
| Care and scheduling constraints | Predictable hours, travel time and availability matching | Childcare availability and safety require real provision |
| Unreliable self-employment income | Repeat customer work and support through completion | Training alone does not create purchasing power |

## 2. What broad enterprise surveys actually establish

### A strong older national baseline

FinScope MSME South Africa 2024 used 4,027 face-to-face interviews and a weighted national design covering different degrees of formality. Startup challenges included sourcing money (50%), cash flow (46%) and insufficient customers (43%). Its growth-obstacle chart includes crime/theft (32%), markets/competition (27%) and finance costs (19%). These are distinct questions, not a single causal league table. [FinMark Trust, methodology and results](https://finmark.org.za/Publications/FS_MSME_2024_results_launch.pdf)

### A more recent growth signal

The September 2026 Shoprite/World Wide Worx survey covered 800 small businesses. Reporting gives 56.9% identifying market access as a key growth enabler, 39.5% financial support and 29.7% digital tools/platforms. It also notes that orders require working capital and capacity. We inspected reporting and issuer material, but not a complete sampling/weighting appendix: do not claim the result represents every South African SME. [TimesLIVE, 17 September 2026](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/2026-09-17-access-to-customers-barrier-for-smme-growth/)

A Business Partners Q1 2026 release, published in July, reports widespread fuel-related operating pressure and responses involving prices, expenses and supply chains. The public release does not supply enough methodology to treat its percentages as national estimates. [Business Partners release](https://www.businesspartners.co.za/surging-fuel-costs-force-sa-smes-into-survival-mode-as-confidence-cracks/)

**Interpretation:** getting customers, delivering work and being paid are related but separate bottlenecks. Diagnose which one caused the last lost sale before prescribing software.

## 3. Sector evidence and opportunities

### A. Salons and beauty

In a November 2025 Vutivi interview, Eldorado Park salon owner Nokuthula Dube described rent rising from R3,500 to R5,000, squeezing products and assistant pay. This is one owner's account, not an average salon rent increase. The article's percentage wording does not match those amounts; use the amounts if citing it. [Owner interviews](https://vutivibusiness.co.za/business/local-entrepreneurs-feel-the-pinch-as-rent-prices-climb/)

**Candidate intervention:** help an owner calculate whether a paid assistant shift or bookable shared chair covers its true costs, with actual availability and customer commitments. Local customer retention may matter, but a South African salon-wide no-show rate was not established in this research. Validate cancellations, product costs, power/water and repeat visits separately. Software cannot lower the landlord's rent on its own.

### B. Spazas and convenience retail

On the Dot's October 2026 release describes July fieldwork involving 51 face-to-face owner interviews in Western Cape townships. It reports 49% collecting all stock themselves and 57% conditionally willing to use more delivery. Small orders, storage, cash timing and delivery trust matter. This local, commercially commissioned study is useful problem discovery, not national prevalence or proven willingness to pay. [Study release](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/south-africa-spaza-shops-are-ready-for-delivery-303388a)

**Candidate intervention: StockRelay.** Combine confirmed small orders into an affordable collection run, with supplier confirmation, agreed substitutions, itemised receipts and delivery acceptance. Test whether savings exceed delivery and coordination costs. Avoid stocking speculative inventory or advancing credit in the MVP.

### C. Mechanics and repair workshops

AP's June 2025 reporting describes mechanic Themba Maseko losing time obtaining parts. It also profiles MotorSpares Collective, an existing pooled procurement/delivery service. This is a concrete mechanism and a competition warning, not a sector survey. [AP reporting](https://apnews.com/article/0a896cb34e59489296460299b3a5117c)

**Candidate intervention:** coordinate a repair order's approved quote, confirmed parts, collection and customer status. A youth opportunity could be supervised inventory/admin work. Do not assign safety-critical repairs to unqualified people or trust a photograph alone to establish part compatibility. Check existing services before claiming novelty.

### D. Food businesses and restaurants

Yoco's June 2025 interpretation of the Eat Out/Yoco 2024 restaurant survey highlights input prices, inventory/admin and staff development. The public article does not provide a usable sample denominator; the underlying full report was not inspected. Vutivi separately reports takeaway owner Mpho Phiri reducing staff under rent/payroll pressure. [Restaurant survey commentary](https://www.yoco.com/za/blog/eat-out-yoco-extra-time/) · [Takeaway interview](https://vutivibusiness.co.za/business/local-entrepreneurs-feel-the-pinch-as-rent-prices-climb/)

**Candidate intervention:** confirmed meal preorders tied to purchasing and production, with a simple true-margin calculation. Test whether predictable orders help more than another delivery channel. Food safety, storage and collection windows are operational requirements, not features an LLM can certify.

### E. Clothing producers, uniform makers and fashion brands

In an August 2026 interview, Thabiso of T-Squared describes difficulty obtaining appropriate equipment and material suppliers, and the value of production control. The interview also describes local garment training/employment. It is a single founder's account, but directly connects production constraints and skills pathways. [HYPE interview, 24 August 2026](https://hypemagazine.co.za/2026/08/24/in-conversation-with-thabiso-from-t-squared/)

**Candidate intervention:** turn a confirmed uniform order into a production plan, material requirements, supervised tasks, quality checks and delivery evidence. Longer term, coordinate compatible workshops around standardised orders. Validate buyer demand, tolerances and responsibilities before attempting distributed production.

### F. Clothing retail

Vutivi's clothing-trader interview describes a premises-versus-visibility trade-off when moving home. Retail and garment manufacturing should not be treated as the same business. [Clothing trader account](https://vutivibusiness.co.za/business/local-entrepreneurs-feel-the-pinch-as-rent-prices-climb/)

**Candidate intervention:** paid preorders or short local collection/pop-up windows that test actual demand before premises commitments. Inventory forecasting needs transaction history; a new seller cannot obtain reliable forecasts from an empty dashboard.

### G. Manufacturing

BER's September 2026 release puts manufacturing confidence at 27 in Q3, down from 31 in Q2. It describes easing cost pressure in Q3. This is a broad manufacturing indicator, not a small-factory survey or a percentage of firms closing. Detailed current capacity constraints were not publicly established from the summary. [BER manufacturing survey](https://www.ber.ac.za/Documents/Index/Absa-Manufacturing-Survey)

**Candidate intervention:** confirmed-order coordination and auditable production milestones. Shared capacity or procurement could help specific firms, but idle machines, suitable subcontractors and spare labour must be observed rather than inferred from weak confidence.

### H. IT services and software

OfferZen's 2026 report draws on over 2,200 tech professionals. It is useful workforce context, but employee sentiment is not a survey of IT business owners or unemployed entrants. Do not turn salary dissatisfaction into an SME customer-acquisition statistic. [OfferZen report](https://www.offerzen.com/resources/developer-salary-benefits-report)

**Candidate intervention:** a buyer-approved, paid small project with specific deliverables, access controls, acceptance tests and a referenceable result. Test genuine buyer budgets, repeat work and scope disputes. Avoid manufacturing endless unpaid assessments or selling unverified junior work as expert delivery.

### I. Creative freelancers and agencies

Safrea's 2026 report has 413 voluntary responses collected in 2025/26 through digital channels, largely from South Africa. It finds wide rate variation and underpricing at the lower end. It explicitly uses a non-probability convenience sample; its pricing figures are indicative, not prescribed tariffs. [Safrea full report, methodology pp. 4–6](https://safrea.co.za/wp-content/uploads/Safrea-Rates-Report-2026-v2.pdf)

**Candidate intervention:** translate a client's brief into agreed deliverables, revision limits, cost-aware pricing and milestone acceptance. A generic AI content generator may add competitive pressure to the same people the app claims to help. Investigate payment reliability directly; this report is principally about rates, not a national late-payment study.

### J. Cleaning and domestic services

SweepSouth's 2025 report used over 5,000 responses collected in June/July through SMS and WhatsApp. Median reported domestic-work earnings were R3,635 per month against its R4,500 basic cost basket. The sample is not a national probability sample; it concerns workers rather than cleaning-company owners. [SweepSouth report](https://sweepsouth.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_DomesticWorkerReportV3.pdf)

**Candidate intervention:** dependable repeat bookings, travel-aware scheduling and visible net earnings. Compare total paid hours and transport costs; more bookings can still mean worse earnings if travel and unpaid gaps expand.

### K. Small-scale agriculture

A June 2026 Jozini study uses focus groups and semi-structured interviews. Farmers describe poor implementation, coordination, maintenance, communication and reach of market-access programmes. The abstract does not establish sample size or fieldwork date; those remain unverified here. Findings apply to the studied setting, not every farmer. [Peer-reviewed study](https://foodsystemsjournal.org/index.php/fsj/article/view/1520)

**Candidate intervention:** verified buyer commitments with agreed grades, collection arrangements and responsibility for rejection. This needs functioning logistics and quality control. Another produce listing site will not repair roads or create cold storage.

### L. Construction, trades and hardware

BER's Q3 2026 public summaries describe mixed building activity and a sharp fall in civil-construction activity. Its coverage includes contractors, subcontractors, professionals and parts of the building supply chain. Neither confidence nor broad activity establishes the dominant pain of a local small contractor. [BER building/construction surveys](https://www.ber.ac.za/Documents/Index/Building-and-Construction)

**Candidate intervention:** accepted scope, materials schedule, progress records and variation approval. Validate late payment, tender access, tools and customer acquisition separately. The public summaries inspected do not substantiate a current sector-wide late-payment percentage.

### M. Tourism and experience businesses

A January 2026 article includes a named tour-business founder describing a move into online coaching to make income steadier. It also reports Xero's 2025 cash-flow findings, with a methodology not established in that article. Treat the founder account as evidence of one seasonal adaptation. [Interview and survey reporting](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/sa-small-businesses-rebuilt-cash-flow-models-to-drive-growth-in-2025-061265a)

**Candidate intervention:** confirmed group bookings and deposits through an established payment provider, with cancellation terms and scheduling. Test whether extra software is more useful than reliable distribution or buyer partnerships.

## 4. Where the biggest addressable gaps appear

The following is a reasoned product-priority assessment, not a statistical national ranking.

1. **Reliable paid demand:** strong recent broad survey signal. Regular orders may be more valuable than business information. Risk: an app cannot promise buyers into existence.
2. **Order fulfilment:** concrete mechanisms in spaza, repair and clothing evidence. Inputs, time, trust and capacity can stop an existing opportunity becoming revenue. Risk: each sector needs different operations.
3. **Access to the first and next earning opportunity:** youth sources support proximity, affordable access, credible experience and continuity. Risk: employers must offer paid work with realistic requirements.
4. **Cash timing and payment:** broad enterprise evidence supports cash-flow problems. Risk: completion records and reminders cannot make an insolvent customer pay.
5. **Operating costs and physical conditions:** highly material but often require physical provision, policy or bargaining. A digital service should only claim the specific costs it can reduce.

The attractive intersection is **earning continuity through completed real orders**. It links youth outcomes and business outcomes without assuming every owner needs staff or every young person should start a business.

## 5. Ranked concepts for the hackathon

These rankings reflect fit to the requested ambition, evidence and demonstrability. They are not official judging scores; the exact current rubric has not been verified in this research.

| Rank | Concept | Why it deserves consideration | Main condition / risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **OrderBridge: a real order becomes a supervised paid pathway** | Strong narrative joining customer demand, execution and credible experience; measurable business and youth outcomes | Must find an owner with a real order, a delegable bottleneck and budget |
| 2 | **StockRelay: small orders, shared collection, verifiable delivery** | Strong recent local interview evidence; concrete savings and visible physical demo | Supplier participation, route economics, existing competition and loss responsibility |
| 3 | **ShiftFit: worthwhile work after travel and scheduling costs** | Direct youth evidence; net-income comparison is immediately understandable | Verified opportunities and access support, rather than speculative listings |
| 4 | **ProofPay: a paid small project with an agreed acceptance contract** | Relevant to IT and creative work; clear scope, revision and payment workflow | Genuine customers and disputes; crowded freelance/project-tool market |
| 5 | **DemandFirst Factory: combine compatible producers around a confirmed order** | More ambitious coordination innovation; can extend garment production | Quality, buyer trust, capacity verification and coordination overhead |
| 6 | **ReverseFactory: surplus into approved products** | Strong visual potential and circular-economy story | Current evidence does not establish dependable material supply, buyer demand or economics |

OrderBridge and DemandFirst Factory can become one long-term product. The first hackathon build should demonstrate one workshop and one order. Multi-workshop optimisation is a later extension, rather than a dependency on day one.

## 6. Recommended problem statement

> Young people need credible paid experience, while small businesses need reliable ways to complete customer work. How might we turn a verified customer order and an owner's specific execution bottleneck into a nearby, supervised, fairly paid opportunity—with clear acceptance, payment status and portable evidence of the work?

This is a product hypothesis synthesised from separate evidence bases. The research does not yet show what proportion of owners have a youth-suitable task or a sustainable budget for it.

### The distinctive mechanism: an order with an executable plan

Each order is represented as a dependency graph: customer commitment → inputs → work steps → inspections → delivery → payment. The product identifies the next blocked step and offers only actions that could actually unblock it.

A youth task exists only when the owner approves its scope, supervision, budget and acceptance conditions. Qualification-sensitive tasks stay with qualified workers. The value proposition is completion of commercially useful work and a credible record of the participant's contribution.

### Illustrative demo: one uniform producer

All amounts, order sizes and outcomes in this scenario are demo fixtures, not research findings.

1. An owner records a voice note about a confirmed uniform order and photographs its size list.
2. The app extracts quantities and deadlines. It highlights uncertainty; the owner corrects and confirms every commercially important field.
3. A production checklist exposes a packing/size-reconciliation bottleneck. The owner creates a supervised paid shift with explicit hours and budget.
4. A nearby participant sees the expected pay, travel cost estimate, supervisor, required abilities and acceptance checklist.
5. Offline, the participant scans package QR codes and records counts; duplicate or missing items are flagged by deterministic checks.
6. The owner signs off acceptance. The payment provider or owner confirms actual payment separately; task completion is never displayed as payment received.
7. A portable work record states exactly what was done, when, for whom and who verified it. It is not an accredited qualification.
8. A visual order view shows which dependencies were resolved and which remain blocked.

### Technology with a purpose

- **Speech and languages:** voice capture and read-back in languages tested with actual pilot users. Validate code-switching, names, quantities and noise. Do not promise equal-quality support for every official language.
- **Document/computer vision:** assist with order lists and packing evidence. Human confirmation for extracted information; physical quality remains supervised.
- **Optimisation:** schedule dependencies and assign tasks within ability, time, travel and budget constraints. Use explicit rules and a constraint solver before opaque candidate ranking.
- **Offline operation:** cached task details, local checklists, queued evidence and visible sync state. Supplier confirmations, matching updates and payment verification still need connectivity.
- **Verifiable records:** signed acceptance plus a QR-linked verification view. Protect customer data and allow participant consent/revocation where appropriate. A signature proves who approved a statement, not that every statement is true.
- **AI assistants:** optional extraction and explanation. Owner approval is mandatory for orders, task budgets and commitments. Multiple agents are only justified if they improve a tested workflow.
- **Spatial view:** the Gods Eye View direction could inspire an optional presentation of workshops, routes and blocked orders. Build the actual worker interface around a clear next action. A visual map does not establish real capacity, live stock or surveillance data.

### What makes the demonstration convincing

The judge sees an order move through states, a specific blocked step resolve, an actual acceptance record and a clear payment status. A camera, voice note or map earns its place by changing that outcome. Show the same task working through loss of connectivity and later synchronisation.

For a hackathon, use an explicitly labelled payment sandbox. Live payouts, custody of funds, supplier contracts and official FNB integration are separate partner-dependent work. Do not imply the sponsor has approved an integration.

## 7. Evidence to gather before choosing the vertical

Start with 6–10 owners split across two plausible sectors and 8–12 young people near them. This is discovery, not a representative survey. Seek voluntary, redacted artefacts; avoid collecting IDs, customer phone numbers or unnecessary bank information.

### Owner interview: inspect a recent event

1. Tell me about the last paid order you delivered late, refused or lost. When was it?
2. What exactly blocked it: no buyer, no deposit, inputs, time, skills, premises, transport or payment?
3. Can you show a redacted order, receipt or message that illustrates that sequence?
4. How much time or money did the blocker cost? What did you actually do about it?
5. Was there a task you could safely delegate? Who could supervise and accept it?
6. What have you previously paid for similar help? From which existing budget?
7. What would make the work unacceptable? Who handles rework, missing items or disputes?
8. Which current tool, supplier or person already solves part of this? Why is it insufficient?
9. Would this replace an existing worker's hours or add capacity? Check displacement.
10. Can we test one real, paid task on an upcoming order?

### Youth interview: inspect the last earning attempt

1. Walk through your last applications, interview or paid piece of work.
2. What transport, data and document costs did you actually pay?
3. Which times and locations are possible given care, safety and other commitments?
4. What work can someone already verify you have done? Who verified it?
5. When was payment promised, when did it arrive and were costs deducted?
6. What would make a local opportunity untrustworthy?
7. What instructions or checks would help you succeed at this specific task?
8. Would the proposed shift leave you better off after travel and unpaid time?

### Selection and rejection rules

- Choose a vertical only if owners produce repeated examples of the same blocker, not merely agreement with a pitch.
- Prefer an upcoming confirmed order over hypothetical future demand.
- Continue only if the owner can approve a paid task and supervision without shifting unacceptable risk onto the participant.
- Reject the youth-task concept if the actual bottleneck is solely working capital, scarce expert skill or lack of customers.
- If collection dominates, investigate StockRelay. If buyer demand dominates, secure a buyer or reduce the scope to a demand experiment. If premises dominate, an order workflow may be the wrong intervention.

## 8. Measure useful outcomes

| Outcome | Measurement | Common misleading substitute |
|---|---|---|
| Participant benefit | Actual received pay minus travel/data costs; paid hours; repeat paid work | Registrations or certificates generated |
| Owner benefit | Confirmed orders completed, rework, owner time saved, contribution after task costs | Gross order value labelled profit |
| Trust | Accepted/rejected tasks, disputes, payment time, verifier checks | An unexplained AI trust score |
| Offline usability | Tasks completed without a connection; sync failures; data transferred | A cached home screen labelled fully offline |
| Inclusion | Completion by language, device and access needs in the pilot | Counting languages listed in settings |

Before/after comparisons in a tiny pilot are suggestive, not causal proof. Record baseline conditions and failures, and disclose subsidies. Do not call a one-off paid shift a permanent job.

## 9. Claims to avoid in the pitch

- “The biggest problem for every business is X.” The evidence does not support a universal answer.
- “56.9% cannot find customers.” The 2026 question concerns a reported growth enabler.
- “Youth job search costs R1,469 today.” That figure comes from a 2023 sample.
- “All spazas prefer cash because they are digitally illiterate.” The local study suggests a more nuanced trust issue.
- “AI proves competence / guarantees quality / ensures payment.” Verification and real operations remain necessary.
- “This is the first-ever platform.” Existing procurement, employment and freelance services already cover parts of the workflow.
- “We have created jobs” when the result is a demo or a one-off subsidised task.
- “This wins every judging criterion.” Confirm the actual rubric and demonstrate evidence against it.

## Final recommendation

Pursue **OrderBridge** as the most ambitious combined concept, conditional on finding a real order and a paid, supervised task. Keep **StockRelay** as the strongest immediate alternative supported by recent sector interviews. Update the ranking after direct discovery; the most useful result may be rejecting an attractive idea that owners cannot economically use.
