# R3 - Youth savings, access, language: evidence file

Researcher run date: 2026-10-05. Doctrine: extract, do not infer. Every figure below is a literal quote (in quotation marks) or is labelled `COMPUTED` (arithmetic done in Python from quoted inputs). Anything I could not find is marked NOT FOUND. Anything I did not research is marked NOT RESEARCHED.

**Tool limits that shaped this file.** The session's WebSearch budget (200 of 200) ran out mid-run, so sections 5 to 7 were completed with direct fetches of primary pages and public literature APIs (Europe PMC, OpenAlex, Crossref, PMG parliamentary records) instead of general search. Several PDFs were read by extracting text locally. Where a source was a secondary news page, a "Secondary" flag is in the caveat column.

Source-quality key: **P** = primary (regulator, statistics office, Parliament, gazette, peer-reviewed paper or the dataset publisher). **S** = secondary (news or trade press quoting a primary). **V** = vendor or self-selected survey.

## Gaps and surprises

**Largest gap.** No evidence anywhere in this file ties any "future self" device to actual saving by South African young people. Everything behavioural is US/UK/Mexico, and the only real-money result (Mexico, nearly 50,000 savers) moved one-time contribution rates from 1.5% to 1.7%. "Future You" (N=344) measured anxiety and future self-continuity only, and its authors report agency, optimism and future consideration "were not significantly impacted".

**Surprises and traps**

1. The "significant" Hershfield Study 1 result is one-tailed only (COMPUTED two-tailed p = 0.073); Study 2 is p = .056. The effect sizes (d about 0.5 to 0.8) come from samples of 21 to 50, with hypothetical money.
2. The one "failure to replicate" I found (Stockdale and Sanders 2020, n = 219) tests textual aging primes, not avatars. No independent replication of the avatar studies was found.
3. Two-pot repeat use is the strongest local finding: Alexforbes reports 67% of 2025 claimants claimed again in 2026 and 31% claimed in all three tax years; Momentum reports 62% of claims since 1 March 2026 were third withdrawals. Reported top reasons are basic living costs, debt and emergencies. This is a product-need signal (cash-flow buffer), not an "I don't care about the future" signal.
4. SARS's own two-pot tax numbers do not reconcile (R12.9bn for 2024/25 per the Commissioner vs "R11.9 billion" quoted as "last year's" in the 1 Apr 2026 release). The latest national withdrawal total I could source is about R57bn and almost 4 million withdrawals (20 Jun 2025, via a radio report of the Commissioner); a SARS-published total after 31 Jan 2025 (R43.42bn) was NOT FOUND.
5. Household saving is negative: -1.3% of disposable income (Q1 2026) and -1.6% (Q2 2026, aggregator). Debt is 61.3% to 62.2% of disposable income. NCR impaired records were 36.05% of 29.24 million credit-active consumers (June 2025); later quarters were not retrievable.
6. Youth credit is bank-light and lender-heavy: Gen Z took 53% of non-bank personal-loan originations in Q1 2026 (TransUnion), but 23% of bank personal loans. The popular "32% use a mashonisa" figure is a lender-run survey (n = 4,872) and should not be quoted as a national rate.
7. The "50 to 60% first-year dropout" line is vendor marketing. DHET's cohort data: 14.8% first-year dropout (2011 contact cohort), 35.9% for distance, and "a third" never graduate in 10 years.
8. SASL: three official-sounding dates and counts conflict. Signed 19 July 2023 (Presidency, Stats SA). Wikipedia claims a 26 June 2026 proclamation (UNVERIFIED, no second source). Users: 12,400 (Census home language) vs 310,247 with severe hearing difficulty vs 500,000 to 1.5 million (historical Deaf estimates) vs 4 million (all deaf and hard of hearing). Deaf unemployment "70%" traces to a 2018 DeafSA statement, not Stats SA. SASL machine translation scores BLEU-4 1.35 on the only public-style corpus, so avatars are not production-ready.
9. Student beds: Parliament's own university figures do not reconcile (components sum to 535,400 vs a stated 527,497), and the TVET "shortfall" appears as both 76,574 and 171,418 in the same record set. Pick one definition and cite it.
10. NSFAS N+ rule: it exists (N+2 allows five years on a three-year degree), but I found no count of students excluded by it.

**NOT FOUND (list):** FNB/RMB two-pot data; youth share of two-pot withdrawals; SARS two-pot total after 31 Jan 2025 from SARS itself; NCR Credit Bureau Monitor after June 2025; NCR age tables for unsecured lending; a representative mashonisa prevalence; direct independent replication of the 2011 avatar studies; any published outcome for Merrill Lynch "Face Retirement"; any bank SASL service or Deaf banking-complaint count; Stats SA Deaf-specific unemployment; a SASL avatar product or Kaggle/Hugging Face SASL dataset; DHET first-year dropout for 2018/2019 cohorts; TVET cohort completion; N+ exclusion counts; national student mental-health prevalence; national SA economic-abuse prevalence; any survey showing the share of survivors who stay for lack of money; online protection-order usage counts; youth English-comfort-for-finance survey; any study of mother-tongue financial information improving comprehension or uptake.

**NOT RESEARCHED (budget):** the NCR Consumer Credit Market Report age tables; the FinScope 2023/24 youth cuts; the Banking Ombud complaint data; PanSALB or Stats SA language-preference surveys; DHET TVET cohort publications.
