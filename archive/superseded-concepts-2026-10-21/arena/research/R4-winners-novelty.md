What the winners have in common (my synthesis):
1. Four of seven overall winners are health or education (Ambani, Kena, Matric Live, Vula); three are finance/retail-banking (Naked, EasyEquities, Shoprite).
2. Live products with measurable usage beat concepts: Vula cites 2.5m referrals over 11 years (TechCentral https://techcentral.co.za/vula-medical-named-as-south-africas-2025-app-of-the-year/275300/). The FAQ rubric asks for a complete, usable app, market need and a path to revenue.
3. Local context is repeatedly the hook: African-language learning (Ambani), stokvels (StokFella, twice), rural referral (Vula), township safety (Eyerus).
4. Young founders who first won Breakthrough/Youth awards then won overall (Matric Live: Breakthrough 2020, overall 2024; developer was 21 in 2020 per Ventureburn).
5. Note: the bar for the overall award is established products; the hackathon category is a lower bar of "working product built in 72 hours".

### 1c. Judges and what they look for

2026-10-05 snapshot of the Judges page (raw, https://appoftheyear.co.za/judges/). 19 names, no titles, no panel year stated on the page: Karabo Songo, Thembalethu Shangase, Clint O Shea, Siphiwe Ngwenya, Rob McLean, Mashudu Modau, Ellen Fischat, S'onqoba Vuba, Japhet Manda, Gareth Tresling, Ivan Ragasek, Tumelo Mashabela, Freddy Mahhumane, Mandla Mthombeni, Sandiso Sibisi, Papi Rapolai, Tongai Jinda, Mncedisi Mayekiso, Laureen Mxoli.

Roles known from other sources (not from the judges page): Rob McLean is CEO of App Developer Studios and Tumelo Mashabela is MD of TM Attorneys (both per the 2025 hackathon programme PDF); Freddy Mahhumane is CEO of KasiD, a township food-delivery app (IT-Online https://it-online.co.za/?p=284958). That these three sit on the current judging panel is inferred from the names matching, not stated.

Judge quotes about what they look for: NOT FOUND. No named judge's judging philosophy was published in any source read. Closest primary evidence is the published scoring rubric (raw, https://appoftheyear.co.za/faqs/). Round 1: judges load the apps on devices, play with them and score. Round 2: shortlisted finalists; 3 finalists per category; winner announced at the awards evening ("2 judging phases and 1 Awards evening"). Scored questions, paraphrased:
- Product: complete or far from it; polished and behaves as users expect; actually solves the stated challenge; real market need.
- Creativity: genuinely new; creative approach; improves society or business in a new way; appealing, relevant, exciting; a fresh take on an old problem.
- Usability: clarity, simplicity, intuitive next step.
- Sustainability: potential of the team and app; can it go to the next level; path to revenue.
- Impact: how big a problem; how indispensable the app is.
- Presentation: how well the team tells its story and conveys societal relevance.
- Intelligence: can the app learn from patterns and forecast user behaviour.
Submissions must be complete enough that judges can use the app and see its full capability; unpublished apps are acceptable only if judges get remote access and logins.

Other primary statements:
- 2024 process, per Hypertext (https://htxt.co.za/2024/12/education-app-named-2024-fnb-app-of-the-year/): three stages assessing creativity, feasibility, technical execution and impact potential, with live pitches to business and technology leaders (summarised by the fetch tool; wording not verified in raw).
- 2023 hackathon winners were chosen on planning, technical expertise and understanding of a social problem (AppsAfrica https://www.appsafrica.com/?p=12750, summarised).
- FNB spokespeople: Janis Robson (business development head) says winners gain recognition and funding chances (IT-Online 2025 above); Andiswa Bata (FNB Business CEO) framed the 2024 winner as showing the innovative spirit of SA small businesses (Citizen, above). These are sponsor statements, not judging criteria.
- Standard Bank 2026 hackathon (comparator, not FNB): projects judged on innovation, feasibility, potential business impact and ability to scale (raw, https://www.standardbank.co.za/southafrica/news-and-media/newsroom/standard-bank-group-technology-hackathon-2026-tackles-africas-biggest-ai-opportunities).
