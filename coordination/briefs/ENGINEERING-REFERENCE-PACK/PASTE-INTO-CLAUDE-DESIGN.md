## What the learner should experience

A learner enters a believable, beautifully lit inspection workshop. A multipart electric motor sits on a timber-and-metal bench. They can orbit it, choose a named part, see its role, separate and reassemble the machine, observe a clearly labelled conceptual operating animation, then answer a short question. An optional instructor stands beside the bench and helps explain the selected part. The motor remains the main focus.

Use a **three-phase squirrel-cage induction motor** as the teaching example. Keep its construction and explanation consistent: stationary stator with copper windings, squirrel-cage rotor and shaft, bearings, end bells, housing and cooling features. Do not mix permanent-magnet/brush/DC motor explanations into it. Generated mockup geometry is illustrative; correct any inaccurate structure during modelling.

## Required working interactions

1. **Inspect:** drag/touch to orbit, wheel/pinch to zoom within useful bounds, and a reset-camera control. Do not hijack normal page scrolling outside the viewport. Provide labelled camera presets for keyboard users.
2. **Select:** click a visible mesh or select its matching item in the keyboard-accessible parts list. Use the same stable part ID for both. Highlight only that part, update its title/fact/explanation, and offer Focus part. Expose selection with aria-pressed or equivalent DOM semantics. Hide or reposition occluded labels.
3. **Explode:** a continuous 0–100% slider separates groups along intentional local axes, then returns exactly to their saved assembly transforms. Default duration about 650 ms after an explicit action, with eased motion and no oscillation. Handle rapid reversals without cumulative drift. Keep rotor/shaft relationships and bearing pivots coherent. Never merge all semantic parts into one mesh.
4. **Show inside:** hide or clip the outer housing so the rotor and stator can be inspected. Explain that a cutaway is an inspection aid. All remaining visible parts still work with selection.
5. **Operate:** offer play/pause and an explicitly labelled **visual playback speed**, not a simulated real shaft RPM. Show a restrained conceptual rotating-field overlay if it can be explained correctly. The stator stays stationary. The rotor/shaft turn together. Operation requires an assembled motor; pause operation while exploded, and make the reason visible. Pausing freezes the current angle.
6. **Learn:** show a prepared, correct explanation for the selected part and the currently active mode. Offer optional Read aloud only after learner action. If no real AI backend is connected, omit live chat or visibly mark it unavailable; do not simulate an AI answer. The existing KAIRO cloud guide is prepared for a different lesson and must not be silently reused as a motor expert.
7. **Practise:** ask “Which part holds the windings?” Offer Housing, Stator, Rotor and Shaft; accept **Stator**, and explain that its stationary windings create the rotating magnetic field. Do not ask which part stays still with Housing and Stator as competing answers: both stay still. An incorrect choice gets useful feedback and another attempt. Also offer a small field-speed exercise: n_s = 120f/p, f in Hz and p the total even number of poles. At 50 Hz and 4 poles, n_s = 1500 r/min; at 50 Hz and 2 poles, 3000 r/min. Label this as rotating-field synchronous speed, not a prediction of actual induction-motor rotor speed. Use bounded positive inputs and reject invalid values. Do not invent torque, heat, efficiency or a validated physical simulation.
8. **Access:** captions and a text/2D parts alternative remain usable without the avatar, audio or WebGL. Respect system reduced-motion preference and a manual override: camera changes snap, exploded states update without travel, and operation defaults paused. Every essential action must have a keyboard control and clear focus state.
