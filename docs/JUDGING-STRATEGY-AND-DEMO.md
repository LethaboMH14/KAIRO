# KAIRO: material → understanding → demonstrated practice

## What the user is building

KAIRO converts a learner's own material into a source-linked lesson, presents it through appropriate explanations and interactive studios, observes practice, and changes the help offered. The longer-term bridge is study → workplace scenario → learner-controlled evidence portfolio.

The user's important distinction: preferences are contextual. Accounting might benefit from diagrams; management from a case study. A learner can choose an approach, change it, and eventually see recommendations supported by their own outcomes. Do not classify people into permanent learning styles or assume clicks/time prove learning.

The supplied conversation also proposes local-language narration, realistic or expressive avatar teachers, branching workplace stories, recorded simulations, and employer-facing skill evidence. These remain product directions until their specific implementation is tested. A recording is evidence of activity, not automatically a verified skill, accredited credential or hiring guarantee. Employer sharing requires a deliberate learner action and a meaningful assessment process.

## What the criteria make us prioritise

The official [App of the Year FAQ](https://appoftheyear.co.za/faqs/) supports a usable complete solution and the seven categories supplied by the user. This is the published general competition guidance; a current hackathon-specific weighting or eligibility rule is not established by that page.

| Criterion | Product evidence to show | Remaining evidence to gather |
|---|---|---|
| Product | Choose → inspect OCR/text → save reviewed version → create lesson → enter studio → practise → export | Independent device QA, deployment with authentication, actual learner use |
| Creativity | A document becomes an interactive learning session with inspectable source passages and responsive help | Compare against alternatives through observed learner tasks; no uniqueness guarantee |
| Usability | One clear next action, useful error recovery, reading alternative, immediate local sample | Timed usability sessions, low-end phone/network measurements |
| Sustainability | Reusable studio templates and bounded model calls; local reading path | Per-lesson operating cost and a tested institutional or learner purchase proposition |
| Impact | Starts with material learners already own; helps check and apply it | Completion, retention and transfer evidence from consenting participants |
| Presentation | Show a wrong answer changing the help, then show the resulting exported record | Rehearse an uninterrupted live demonstration |
| Intelligence | Source-conditioned lesson generation, explicit preferences, mistake-triggered support | Topic-level outcome history and evaluated recommendations; prediction is not yet implemented |

The pasted AI scorecards are opinions with inconsistent arithmetic. Scores such as 68/70, guaranteed wins, instant mass scaling, flawless reasoning and guaranteed employer demand are not evidence. Cloud deployment alone does not establish completion, performance or adoption.

## Demonstration: one visible loop

1. Open My material. Use the authored shopkeeper passage for a repeatable public demonstration, or a consenting learner's document.
2. Inspect the extracted words. Make a correction and save the reviewed text. Explain that the lesson cites this exact revision.
3. Explicitly choose Google Cloud generation. Show the resulting explanation and source chips; open a passage.
4. Enter the existing 3D classroom. Change chapters and show the board changing. Switch to reading view to show the same lesson remains usable.
5. Choose a wrong practice answer. The app explains why help changed and opens the supporting source passage. Retry and explain the reasoning.
6. Add a note. Reload to demonstrate saved browser progress. Export notes with source passages and the practice record.

Describe this accurately as dynamic lesson content in a reusable interactive classroom, not a newly generated 3D world. The local path is extractive reading/source recognition, not AI teaching or mastery assessment.

## Next stages after the source workspace

1. Evaluate lessons against reviewed source material, including wrong student answers and OCR errors.
2. Add topic-level learning history with explicit preferences, transparent suggestions and learner override. Compare outcomes before proposing a format change.
3. Add one workplace scenario with actions, consequences and an assessable rubric. Engineering remains Claude's separate task.
4. Add local-language narration and avatar turn-taking with real language/device tests; keep captions and reading controls.
5. Add opt-in recordings and a private evidence portfolio. Build assessment integrity before calling a result verified.
6. Establish authenticated cloud storage/deployment, deletion controls, operating-cost telemetry and a small learner pilot.
