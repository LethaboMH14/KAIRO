# KAIRO: selected product direction

Decision recorded: 21 October 2026, from the user's explicit selection of the attached discussion. This brief captures the direction; it does not approve every earlier assistant recommendation as a final requirement.

## Central idea

Make complex knowledge tangible and accessible. A learner supplies a textbook passage, PDF, photograph, notes, supported transcript, case or practical problem. KAIRO understands the source, plans a lesson and composes an appropriate interactive environment. The learner explores, changes variables, asks questions, practises and demonstrates understanding.

The core loop is:

Source -> grounded understanding -> lesson plan -> world composition -> guided interaction -> assessment -> adaptation and saved progress.

## Platform and flagship

KAIRO Core provides multimodal understanding, reasoning, retrieval, memory, tools, permissions and context. KAIRO Access is the first deeply developed product, combining experiential learning with accessible digital assistance. Its AI Skills and Practical Work capability also teaches useful workflows for study, coding, research and business, including verification and avoiding generic AI output.

The same runtime supports distinct environments with their own assets, interactions and domain logic:

- **Adaptive Lecture Theatre:** a teacher with synchronized speech, lip movement, gestures, board actions, questions and transitions into practical activities.
- **Engineering Studio:** realistic devices, component selection, disassembly, wiring or flow views, parameter changes, fault scenarios and validated calculations.
- **Law and Advocacy Studio:** a courtroom with role-specific AI characters, evidence, spoken arguments, objections and rubric-based feedback.
- **Anatomy and Health Sciences Studio:** layered anatomical structures, selectable systems and validated educational demonstrations.
- **Industrial and Mining Studio:** equipment, material flows and process scenarios connected to documented industrial principles.
- **Computer Science Studio:** interactive architecture, request traces, algorithms, database operations, CPU and memory concepts.

These six studios remain part of the product vision. A staged implementation sequence must preserve that ambition while identifying honestly which interactions actually work.

## World Composer and technical intent

The World Composer selects a scene, assembles assets, configures valid interactions, connects simulation logic and exposes contextual controls. The teacher operates through structured, validated actions tied to the lesson and scene state.

Reusable components and domain-specific simulation engines provide reliable behaviour. Controlled generation can extend explanations and interactions. Unsupported topics need a useful grounded fallback, such as an accessible diagram or guided explanation.

Reality Mode connects a supported photo, manual or real-world object to an available representation: inspect its exterior, explore its internals and experiment with supported behaviour. Educational models, specific equipment replicas, validated simulations and live connected twins require distinct evidence and labels.

Candidate technologies in the original discussion include React/TypeScript, Three.js/React Three Fiber, Blender/glTF, Python/FastAPI, retrieval, PostgreSQL/pgvector, real-time voice and permissioned tools. These are candidates pending a concrete architecture and tool evaluation. Subscription access must not be assumed to provide application API access.

## Experience and accessibility

The visual target is a distinctive, colourful, polished spatial product with considered lighting, materials, assets, camera movement and responsive interaction. Each studio needs a purposeful visual identity and meaningful actions.

Learners should be able to interrupt, ask contextual questions, request a different explanation, switch supported languages and resume their place. Voice, text, captions, transcripts, keyboard controls, reduced motion and lightweight 2D alternatives support different needs and device constraints.

Gaze assistance, tremor compensation and sign-language capabilities are important ambitions requiring specific implementation and validation. Assessment evidence informs mastery estimates and subsequent lessons. Live retrieval, learner adaptation and separately evaluated model improvements are distinct mechanisms.

## Expansion: Commerce and other workspaces

KAIRO Commerce is a spatial business assistant for spaza shops, stalls, tables, food vendors and other small merchants. A guided phone scan maps trading areas and visible products into a usable spatial representation. Confirmed sales, deliveries, waste and later scans maintain a reconciled inventory ledger.

Observed, ledger and verified quantities must remain distinguishable. Forecasting, supplier comparisons, proposed orders, layout assistance, document support and business administration build on that foundation. External commitments require appropriate user authorization.

Commerce shares perception, spatial interaction, voice, memory and tools with Access, while retaining its own business logic. Opportunity and Enterprise remain further platform directions.

## Commercial intent and evidence

The discussion proposes institutions and workforce-training buyers, with optional consumer services and free essential accessibility. Prices and revenue examples are hypotheses to test, not accepted pricing or forecasts.

The intended initial competition positioning is access to higher education, with practical skills and work readiness as related benefits. Current rules, dates, research statistics, competitor capabilities, prices, licences and integration permissions require verification before external use.

## First proof of the product

An unfamiliar supported source should produce a traceable lesson, a suitable interactive environment, contextual teacher responses, a correct simulation or structured scenario, a meaningful assessment and saved progress. A learner should have usable alternatives to voice and 3D, and recover from failed requests.

This is the first engineering proof within the broader vision. Visual quality, educational correctness, accessibility, performance, operating cost and measured learning benefit each need their own evidence.
