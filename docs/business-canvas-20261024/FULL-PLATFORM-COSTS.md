24 October 2026. **Pricing hypotheses and cost envelopes, not accepted quotes, actual bills, revenue or proof of viability.** This document expands the earlier learning-slice model to include the wider product. It does not silently retrofit its margin/funding figures to unbuilt features. The R35 Core and R59 Connected prices are distinct proposals with different costs and scope; the earlier workbook is **Core only**. R59 is not a claimed market-clearing price. Why institution + company offers belong: [Coursera Campus](https://www.coursera.org/campus) publicly links higher education to role preparation/portfolios; [Coursera Business](https://www.coursera.org/business/compare-plans) separates team licences and tailored enterprise services. [Udemy Business](https://business.udemy.com/plans/) lists an annual per-user Team plan atUS$360 and enterprise pricing by sales; its [billing FAQ](https://business-support.udemy.com/hc/en-us/articles/18981435834775-Team-Plan-pricing-and-purchasing-FAQ) explains annual upfront payment, location-dependent price/taxes and seat rules. These establish packaging patterns, not comparable KAIRO content quality, local affordability or demand. No Coursera numeric price was assumed from its dynamically loading page. Custom work, SSO/integrations and enterprise support should not be bundled into a cheap seat price without costing.

## How payment must actually work

**Personal:** transparent plan/allowance/tax basis → explicit recurring consent → provider checkout → verified server notification and authoritative amount/currency/account → idempotent ledger → entitlement → receipt. Failed/duplicate payments, renewal, cancellation, grace period, refunds and chargebacks need tests. No card data in KAIRO; no access based solely on browser redirect. Payfast is a researched candidate, not integrated: [official subscription integration guidance](https://support.payfast.help/portal/en/kb/articles/what-are-the-integration-options-for-subscriptions-20-9-2022).

**Institution:** agreed scope and data/content contract → purchase order/authorised signer → tax invoice where applicable → verified roster and licence term → bank/EFT reconciliation or verified provider payment → agreed service start/credit terms → seat/usage reporting → renewal quote. Paying for 250 seats does not permit 250 uploads/second or arbitrary GPU calls. Do not charge the same learner again as B 2 C for a sponsored entitlement. Manage seat replacement/withdrawal/capacity rules explicitly.

**Company:** subscription invoice plus separately invoiced custom development milestones. An example 20%/40%/40% milestone split requires contract acceptance criteria and enough working capital for delivery. Received deposits/unearned annual subscriptions are liabilities until earned; ongoing licences are recognised across the term. Tax, cancellation and company training accreditation claims require professional review. Company reports cannot expose another tenant's training manuals or unconsented learner material.

**Implementation status:** no checkout, production billing/entitlement ledger, company tenant or collected invoice is evidenced. Plan/billing interfaces can be implemented separately after the price/usage contract is published. This documentation and skill installation did not activate payments.

Example expanded active learner-month:4 lesson/help bundles,20 complex PDF pages,10 listening minutes,5,000 translated chars to one target,2,500 premium voice chars, tiny explicit embedding/retrieval/vision reserves, basic infrastructure and 2 support minutes. At budget R20/USD with 15% vendor-tax reserve, **service cost R21.79**, plus **R4.17** expected consumer payment/refund fees. R49 gross yields R41.33 net after VAT/refunds, leaving **R15.37** contribution before shared costs. Contrast original smaller Core usage: **R28.02** contribution. This is why one low price must not imply every modality is unlimited.

Institution Connected example: same expanded services but 1 support minute and 60% active licensed seats → **R47.13 contribution per R59 licensed seat/month** before shared costs/acquisition. Workforce example:8 bundles,20 complex pages,30 listening minutes,20,000 translated chars,8,000 voice chars and 5 support minutes; at 80% active seats, **R111.27 contribution per R149 licensed employee/month**. Neither activity nor support distributions have been measured. Basic controls/reading/signing choices are not per-disability billing. Heavy workloads consume pooled or separately agreed budgets; warnings/approval precede overage, rather than surprise charges.

Stress:200 complex pages at$0.01/page alone cost R46 after FX/tax reserve; large documents can erase the whole R49 plan margin. 80,000 premium voice characters cost more than that subscription before support or any other work. Translation fanout, repeated imports/vision, streaming audio, idle GPU replicas and abusive background loops need separate caps. Exact stress figures and contributions are reproduced in `full-platform-results.json`.

## Development is a real cost

An illustrative fuller-platform monthly delivery capacity budget allocates R35,000 product/engineering, R35,000 AI/backend, R30,000 frontend/3D, R15,000 educational review, R15,000 language/SASL review, R10,000 QA/security/ops =**R140,000**. Add 20% loaded-employment/contract reserve =**R168,000**. Add R6,000 baseline cloud/staging/monitoring, R3,000 development tools, R6,000 legal/privacy/insurance reserve, R10,000 sales/marketing, R2,000 devices/connectivity, R3,000 data-rights/source-curation =**R198,000/month** before volume-linked service costs and bespoke projects.

These are **capacity allocations/budget assumptions**, not verified South African salary quotes, six fully hired staff, guaranteed contractor availability or full insurance/audit coverage. The 20% load is a reserve, not a statutory rate. Dedicated learner support in the unit costs is marginal service capacity; the QA/ops allocation is shared product/operations work. Do not count the same employee's support hours twice. Confirm actual role hours/benefits/employer taxes/tool seats and increase staffing at measured capacity thresholds. USDFX/provider-tax reserves remain provisional; no input-tax recovery assumed.

Bounded remaining build estimate: profile/evidence/adaptation 160 person-hours; role/skill alignment 80; approved opportunities/freshness 100; multimodal review 120; listening/language 80; bounded signed learning 100; tenant/payment/security 120; acceptance/release 80. Total**840 person-hours**, at hypothetical R300 loaded/hour =R252,000, plus 25% risk reserve =**R315,000**. This excludes continuous SASL, broad simulation catalogue, unknown enterprise integrations and external legal/provider/community lead times. It is a rough scoping budget, not measured remaining effort or an elapsed-date promise. If those hours are already paid through the monthly team allocation, **do not add R315,000 again as extra payroll**. Outsourced work needs separate quotes and defined exclusions.

## Custom company-training project example

Suppose a customer requests one new reviewed equipment/workplace module: engineering 60 h×R350=R21,000; instructional design 24 h×R250=R6,000; domain review 20 h×R450=R9,000; QA/accessibility 16 h×R250=R4,000. Direct delivery=**R40,000**. Add 25% scope-risk allowance→R50,000. At target 30% project contribution, quote=R50,000/(1−.30)=**R71,429 ex VAT**. Merely adding 30% markup would give a different margin. All hours/rates/acceptance/rights are assumptions, not an employer quote.

The licence pays for repeated platform service; this project fee pays for specific approved content/scenario creation. Maintenance, major source changes, translations, signer videos, new 3D assets, safety validation and integrations require explicit allowance/change-control. A company-paid project is services revenue, not ARR, and can consume the same engineers needed for the platform. Do not count project staff cost again if already covered as nonincremental internal capacity; model its consumed hours/opportunity cost instead.

## Does the bigger business sustain itself?

At the illustrative **fuller** R198,000 fixed monthly budget,200 consumer payers +1,000 institution Connected seats +100 employee seats +1,000 free active users produce about**R82,166 monthly net recurring revenue** against**R220,090 costs**, a loss around**R137,924/month**. A larger hypothetical mix—500 consumer payers +5,000 Connected seats +500 employee seats +2,000 free active—produces about**R390,165 revenue** and**R291,721 costs**, leaving **R98,444 before income tax**.

Neither mix is a forecast or demonstrated ability to serve that many learners. They show scale/price sensitivity only. They exclude new-contract onboarding/setup/sales shocks, unpaid invoices/chargebacks, bespoke project revenue/cost, full scale-related staffing changes, debt/tax and continuous SASL R&D. The larger run rate is not proof of profit or market demand. At R35 for the same expanded institutional usage, margin falls to R23.13/seat; retaining a clear, lower-cost Core offer and testing a properly scoped fuller contract is more coherent than silently promising everything at R35.

Before funding/launch, build actual cohort cash schedules for each accepted institution/company term, staffing step plan, service-level obligations, collections, retention, tax and rights. The earlier R470 kbuffered Core forecast is **not** the funding needed for this broader platform. Conversely R198 kmonthly capacity is not the current actual cloud bill or founder cash spending. Start with the bounded pilot, measured value/cost and a funded scope; authorise expenditure only against actual resources.

## What must be clear in the canvas and pitch

Independent arithmetic review found no blocker. The mixed run rates assume institution/company invoices are paid by bank transfer with no separately modelled provider fee; actual bank/admin costs and collection delays must enter the future cash model. They are steady-state monthly illustrations, not annual cash forecasts or evidence of collected revenue.

Product/solution: personalised learning → skill evidence → role/opportunity alignment, with multilingual/multimodal/SASL ambition. Buyers: individual, institution, company training and future consented employer workspace. Revenue: recurring licence versus setup versus custom services, with tax/term/seat count. Costs: service meters, shared development/review, sales/implementation and specialist validation. Impact: access/learning/opportunity/employment are separate measurable outcomes. No guarantee of jobs, bursaries, accepted prices or model accuracy. These distinctions strengthen the business story rather than shrinking it.
