## 1. Problem statement (backed by evidence)

Learners can have course material but still struggle to understand it, apply it, demonstrate a useful skill or see how it connects to a career. They also have to navigate role requirements, projects, certificates, bursaries/NSFAS and application deadlines across separate services. Language, disability, device/data limitations and limited practical access can add barriers. KAIRO is being built to connect personalised learning, practical evidence and relevant opportunities, initially for adult engineering and computing/Information Systems learners, then institution and company training environments. Local frequency, severity and willingness to pay still need direct research.

South Africa’s public HEIs recorded 1,115,580 enrolments in 2024 (DHET PSET 2024). Stats SA’s GHS 2024 reports 75.6% household mobile internet access but 17.4% fixed internet at home. These support an access-conscious educational focus, not an assumed paying market. Retrieval/feedback research informs our teaching approach. Our intended contribution to unemployment is to help learners build relevant, inspectable working and prepare for opportunities; learning gains, successful applications and employment effects must be measured separately rather than promised.

## 2. Solution & features

KAIRO’s full journey is: learner profile/course/year/goals and optional confirmed CV → authorised multimodal material → reviewed, cited lesson curated to the person and learning objective → KAIRO teaching in a relevant studio or reading view → practice, feedback and adaptation → saved evidence/skill profile → sourced role requirements and a gap-specific learning/project/certificate plan → relevant jobs, bursaries/NSFAS, hackathons and updates → learner-controlled applications and employer sharing. Spoken-language access, listening and SASL are part of the intended journey. Companies can use approved internal material and competency requirements for role-specific workforce training.

The current foundation includes private materials, cited editable lessons, prepared motor/pump/API activities, saved working, synthetic course profiles, manual role preparation and CV drafting. Full adaptive integration, visual/audio understanding, fresh opportunity feeds, automated role alignment, employer/company workspaces and validated language/SASL services remain delivery work. Current camera tracking is not SASL translation. These are central planned capabilities, not removed from the product or claimed as already validated.

## 3. Unique value proposition

“Read it. See it. Try it. Show what you can do.” KAIRO is being built to connect how a person learns with the skills and opportunities they want to pursue: personally curated explanations, practical environments, evidence of attempted work and a clear next step toward a sourced role requirement. Institutions get a scoped learning-to-opportunity service; companies get training aligned to their approved tasks and competencies. This connected, accessible pathway is the value proposition—not a guaranteed grade, job or bursary. We will test its advantage against existing study and career-support workflows.

## 4. Unfair advantage

Our intended advantage is a trusted map connecting reviewed sources, course concepts, practical tasks, learner-owned evidence and current role requirements, together with reusable instructor/company-reviewed activities and locally validated language/access experiences. KAIRO’s teacher identity and existing learning/studio foundations support that work. Defensibility must grow through rights-cleared content, effective task design, consented outcome evidence and institutional/employer trust—not through possessing a generic LLM or student data. No exclusive moat, validated matching dataset, partner agreement or SASL model is claimed today.

## 5. Customer segments

Primary users: adult engineering and computing/Information Systems students; private individuals preparing for a role; and employees learning approved company tasks. Institutional learners should see their authorised course/modules; employees should see their company/role environment. Start validation with 20–40 consenting learners in one supported module, including different devices/languages/access needs, rather than pretending the whole national market is served.

Buyer hypotheses: individuals choosing a subscription; departments, student-success/career-support programmes, training providers or sponsors funding learner cohorts; and companies paying for workforce licences and custom training content. A later employer talent workspace serves opt-in matching and evidence review under separate consent/terms. The learner and payer can differ. Sponsored learners are not double-billed, and no employer receives automatic access to private materials or profiles.

## 6. Key metrics

Measure the whole pathway: source/visual/transcript coverage and correction; personalised task completion; independent versus assisted practice and delayed transfer; saved/resumed working; learner-reviewed skill evidence and useful gap-specific next actions; freshness and correctness of role/funding records; understood match explanations; and consented application/interview/employment outcomes. Test spoken-language quality and SASL accessibility with qualified users/reviewers; hand landmarks and fluent-looking text are not success metrics.

Commercial measures by segment: cost per completed activity, support/review hours, contribution per learner/employee, paid conversion, cancellation/refund, institution/company renewal, acquisition cost, custom-project margin and invoice collection. Initial decision targets—not results—are 80% independent task completion in a small usability study, no unresolved critical source/privacy errors, and one paid/formally funded scoped pilot before commercial expansion. Job/unemployment impact needs follow-up evidence; registrations, badges and time spent do not establish it.

## 7. Distribution channels

Begin with permission-based tutor/lecturer, academic-support, disability-support, student-society and career-service introductions. Demonstrate one concept-to-evidence-to-role journey, then an opt-in funded pilot. Reach company L&D/HR/engineering teams through a specific training/competency problem; quote custom content separately. Partner-led programme access, later LMS/SSO integration and useful learner referrals are growth channels to test. Use authorised official job/funding sources; no blanket social-platform scraping promise. FNB, NSFAS, universities and employers are not represented as confirmed partners or buyers.

## 8. Cost structure

The earlier Core model budgets R45,000/month shared cost plus R2,500 acquisition; its assumed R49 plan variable cost is R13.31/paid month, before shared costs. It is not the budget for the entire vision. An illustrative fuller-platform capacity budget is R198,000/month shared development/review/operations/sales costs, plus volume-linked AI, document/layout/vision, listening, translation, voice, retrieval, storage/egress, support and payment costs. A broader R49 usage scenario leaves about R15.37 contribution instead of R28.02. All workload, staffing and price assumptions need measurement/quotes.

A bounded remaining build envelope is 840 person-hours×R300 loaded/hour plus 25% risk=R315,000; do not add it again if already funded through shared staff payroll. Continuous SASL, data rights/annotation, specialist validation, new simulations and enterprise integrations require separate scoped budgets. Credit allowances are excluded from durable economics. The earlier R470,000 buffered Core cash scenario is not full-platform funding; no amount is secured or authorised spending. The detailed service meters, tax reserves, collection timing and downside cases accompany this canvas.

## 9. Revenue streams

Price hypotheses: Personal Core R49/month incl VAT if registered; Institution Core R35/seat/month ex VAT, annually R420/seat +R6,000 setup; a fuller Institution Connected service R59/seat/month ex VAT, annually R708/seat +R15,000 setup. At 250 seats those institutional first-year invoices are R111,000 and R192,000 ex VAT respectively. Company workforce training is a separate candidate R149/employee/month ex VAT, annually R1,788/seat +R15,000 setup;50 employees imply R104,400 ex VAT first year. These are scoped price tests, not sold plans or a forced 250-seat validation pilot.

Custom company/institution training modules are separately quoted project revenue: an illustrative R40,000 direct build/review cost plus 25% risk at 30% target contribution implies R71,429 ex VAT—not ARR. Annual licences are earned over the service period, not all when cash arrives. Sponsorship may fund learner access under an actual agreement; employer matching fees/partnership rewards remain unpriced pending consent, legal and buyer validation. No learner-data sale or placement/funding guarantee. We have no verified revenue, accepted price, FNB reward contract or employer partnership.
