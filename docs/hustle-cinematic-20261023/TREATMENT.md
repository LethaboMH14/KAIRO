# KAIRO — the same windows, a different future

23 October 2026. Concept treatment, not a claim that every depicted capability is live. Root: codex-cinematic-20261023-m, Codex gpt-6 / effort unknown. Design retains Chalk/Moss/Sage, Manrope and the selected C direction. The generated companion is a staging placeholder: use the existing authored KAIRO character in final footage, not a replacement identity.

## The emotional proposition

“I am not incapable; I need a way through.” Start inside a recognisable study moment. Let the learner remain active and capable. KAIRO creates space to think; the learner predicts, changes something, explains and keeps their work. The ending shows possibility and agency rather than an invented job offer or transformed marks.

**Main line:** KAIRO. Put knowledge to work.

The motion has a memory: the same windows first represent uncertainty, then become organised next steps. Keep the learner's desk and camera angle at the start and end. Opening shoulders/eye line can move from tense to engaged without a theatrical despair-to-billionaire transformation.

## The accumulating corner system

Use five persistent positions rather than dozens of moving notifications. No chip crosses the learner's eyes, motor target, source text, captions or optional signing area. At any moment only one chip changes. When a chip becomes relevant, its outline brightens and a single connector reaches the main action, then returns to calm. The rail becomes organised at KAIRO's arrival; it does not imply every issue is solved.

| Opening question | Later visual | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I read it. Can I explain it?” | Explanation and motor prediction | Core lesson/practice proof |
| “No lab?” | Supported motor environment | Illustrative practice; not a replacement for required supervised physical lab training |
| “Which role?” | Evidence → Graduate electrical engineer | Example target role; role preparation does not guarantee employment |
| “Bursaries?” / “NSFAS?” | Funding guidance / check eligibility | Vision unless a verified source-backed flow is demonstrated; no actual deadline or award invented |
| “Access?” / “SASL?” | Captions/reading; “SASL support — in development” | Current reading/captions separate from unvalidated sign recognition/translation |

If the user wants the **advert itself signed**, commission/record a consenting qualified SASL interpreter and have the signing checked by Deaf SASL users. Reserve a steady lower-side picture-in-picture area; avoid tight hand/head crops and camera overlays in that area. A decorative hand-tracking avatar is not SASL. No fake signs, phrase translation or unsupported live signing appear in this mock.

## Cinematic grammar and sound

- **Opening:** restrained handheld intimacy, 50–85mm equivalent framing, shallow depth of field; subtle notification ticks grow into one rhythm. Never use real student rejection/funding records as stock.
- **Reveal:** 100–150ms near-silence before the name, then a warmer chord/soft impact. This punctuation makes the reveal felt without a strobe. Keep the actual voice clear over effects.
- **World entry:** one controlled dolly through the engineering portal, with depth from foreground rim, hero motor and distant studio. A match cut from a round notebook sketch to the motor stator makes the transition meaningful. Limit to one major camera move rather than stacking orbit/whip/zoom simultaneously.
- **Material:** paper edge becomes a lesson pane. Source anchor stays attached when the pane moves. A source excerpt becomes the exact variable label used in the motor exercise.
- **Practice:** split-screen movement shares an axis: a finger/slider change on the right aligns with a working step on the left. Midline evidence tile receives both. Star enters once with a short spring, then rests; particles settle within the same beat.
- **Opportunity:** the evidence tile keeps its shape as it travels toward the role. No slot-machine reward burst or cash animation. The sense of earned progress comes from the demonstrated task.
- **Close:** return to recognisable human eye contact, one controlled focus shift, hold the brand. Use an original or properly licensed sonic signature, not music lifted from reference adverts.

Camera pushes and clip transitions are editorial motion, not product interactions. In the local player playback is user initiated. Supply the static storyboard/transcript as the low-motion alternative; final captions stay readable with sound muted. A future Remotion composition should use frame-driven motion, not CSS animation, so rendered timing is deterministic. Use the animation skill's strong ease-out for entries and ease-in-out for spatial morphs; long marketing camera moves remain gentler than UI feedback. These choices still need playback/feel review.

## What to show in 20 seconds, and what to move into the longer film

The 20-second sell carries one clear motor journey. Corner motifs communicate the wider problem without requiring the audience to read a feature catalogue. A 45–60 second film can give 4–6 seconds each to the wider funding/access/career pathways, pause on sources, demonstrate help/retry and sharing permissions, and end with buyer/partner appeal. A separate real demo should prove upload/review/generation/failure recovery, practice and saved evidence without hiding developer intervention. Do not pretend a diagram of the architecture is evidence of deployment.
