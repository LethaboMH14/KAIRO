## One KAIRO identity and source-grounding rules

Keep KAIRO as one teacher in all UI, captions, error states and future voice controls. Keep the existing visual/avatar identity stable. A language switch can alter what a voice can pronounce and can require a different provider voice; it cannot establish that the *same* voice or person is speaking across languages. Recommended behavior:

1. The current lesson remains readable and source-linked before, during and after speech actions.
2. Speech input stays local until the learner chooses **Send transcript for help** (or equally specific copy) and sees which service receives audio/text. Permission to use a microphone alone is not upload consent.
3. A transcript is an editable hypothesis. Do not automatically replace the original passage, an answer, a source quote or a learner's saved work.
4. A translation is a side-by-side draft. Carry source ID, revision, locator and citation anchors as immutable metadata; do not ask a translator to rewrite citation IDs, quantities, formulae, dates, names or negation.
5. If KAIRO lacks an exact matching target voice, use text/captions. Never silently speak translated content using an unrelated English voice or imply a South African accent that is not present.
6. An interruption must cancel any pending audio request, local playback, stream and stale transcript. Stop every microphone `MediaStreamTrack`; do not claim this erases audio already sent to a remote service.

Source grounding needs explicit checks because ASR can omit negation or a number; a translator can normalize a dialect, alter a technical term or lose scope; and an LLM can then explain the changed text confidently. For each evaluated turn compare (a) original audio reference, (b) transcript, (c) learner edits, (d) translation, and (e) cited original passage. Score critical-entity and negation preservation separately from aggregate WER/BLEU/chrF.

## Pretrained versus fine-tuned

- Start with **frozen pretrained inference**. It answers whether KAIRO's current candidate models can handle the locale and target microphone/room before investing in training.
- For modest labeled datasets, train only an adapter/LoRA or an explicitly supported provider language adaptation after a license/consent gate, source deduplication and speaker-disjoint test design. Keep the base checkpoint, data manifest and hashes fixed.
- Do not fine-tune on Common Voice 27.0 Xhosa/Zulu alone: only 5/9 speakers respectively are listed, with very short totals and very little validated audio. These are useful smoke-test material, not representative classroom evidence.
- The SAMA record shows a CC-BY-4.0 metadata license and five very large files, but Level 0 verification, automatically segmented/transcribed broadcaster podcasts and a mismatch between described languages and exposed file list. Confirm underlying recording permissions, dataset-specific terms and label validity before use; do not download it as part of this research.
- Never use restricted / non-commercial model weights for a commercial product. Whisper repo weights are MIT, but that does not automatically clear training data or guarantee South African quality. The NLLB, MMS and Seamless cards checked here are CC-BY-NC 4.0.
- A classifier for study suggestions should begin as a transparent rule list over learner-selected goals and observed attempts. Do not train an “ability” predictor without consented learner-level outcomes, preregistered evaluation, learner-disjoint holdout, calibration, bias review and human oversight.
