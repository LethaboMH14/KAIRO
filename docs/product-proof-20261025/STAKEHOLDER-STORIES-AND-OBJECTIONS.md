# KAIRO stories for learners, judges, sponsors, mentors and buyers

**Date:** 25 October 2026. **Status:** draft scripts/hypotheses, not observed testimonials, official scoring results, evidence of traction or consent to use any institution's logo. Use alongside PROBLEM-TO-PROOF-OPERATING-SYSTEM.md and latest reality-checked app demo.

## The positioning rule

**Don't lead with “AI, 3D avatars, RAG and six studios.” Lead with the learner's actual moment of difficulty.**

“The learner has the material, but cannot use it yet.” The **cause** must be checked in interviews: often there may be a confusing source, missing prerequisite, lack of feedback, poor connection between theory/practice, time/data limitation, or a disability-related interaction barrier. KAIRO is not a universal cure, and some learners already have effective tutors, PhET or Gemini Notebook.

**Working one-liner:** “KAIRO turns *your reviewed study material* into something you can inspect, practise and return to—not just another AI answer.”

**Better product demo title:** “One difficult motor concept. One source. One real attempt. Your working saved.” Replace “motor” only with a validated scene/task and exact supported source.

## The cold audience's questions

| Listener | What they need to hear first | Show, don't claim | Still to prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learner | “Will this actually help with today's assignment without stealing my answer or data?” | First prompt, cited quote, working example, visible practice and user-controlled save | Real comprehension/return/cost benefit |
| Judge | “What challenge does it address, and can I use it myself right now?” | Permissioned synthetic account, actual source revision, successful whole journey and failure recovery | Device/access/judge availability actually tested |
| Lecturer/tutor | “Can I trust the explanation and see where an unassisted attempt came from?” | Reviewer-controlled source correction, citation click, assistance/attempt separation | Educational accuracy/subject review and LMS workflows |
| Disability/accessibility reviewer | “What is possible when 3D, voice, colour or precise pointer input is unusable?” | Keyboard/reader route, subtitles, reduced motion, large text on actual device | Representative user-reviewed accessibility, SASL as separate specialist assessment |
| FNB sponsor | “Does this fit accessible higher education and make the benefit credible?” | Learner journey, data/network tradeoffs, problem validation and one measured success | Funding/pricing, no fictional sponsor integration |
| Institution buyer | “Who supports the staff and data, what are the learning costs and security terms?” | Scoped cohort, educator review process, consent boundaries, published technical risk/cost report | Procurement, pilot interest and budget |
| Investor | “What are your wedge, alternatives, repeat use, defensibility and unit economics?” | Specific supported segment, comparable user task, documented variable AI+speech cost, retention instrumentation and domain assets | Revenue/traction, willingness-to-pay, content rights, durable data moat |

## Script A — 15-second explanation to anyone (proposed)

“Have you ever read a page, watched an explanation, and still not been able to solve the question? KAIRO is designed to connect your checked notes to a clear explanation, something you can try, and practice you can keep. We want to make understanding easier to check, not just answers easier to generate.”

## Script B — 45-second product/pitch narrative (proposed)

“Picture a university learner looking at a motor diagram. The notes describe what happens, but changing the frequency or seeing a fault still feels abstract. They can search, ask AI or find a video, yet their lesson, simulation and practice live in separate places. KAIRO's hypothesis is simple: start with **their reviewed source**, show an explanation linked to that exact text, then let them predict a meaningful change in a validated scene, try a question and keep their real attempt. If the 3D view isn't suitable, use a readable alternative. The test isn't whether our avatar is beautiful; it's whether an unfamiliar learner can do the task, trust the source and return to their working.”

## Script C — Sponsor or institution conversation (proposed)

“We are proposing a narrow pilot for consenting adult learners in one technically demanding module. The problem we want to test is whether they can connect specific course material to application and get feedback without losing source provenance or their first attempt. Instead of selling 'AI tutoring for everyone', we would supply reviewable source-to-practice activities with explicit data controls and meaningful metrics: independent task completion, source errors, repeat use, failure recovery, phone/data burden and educator review time. We are not claiming measured grade improvement or institutional endorsement. We would like to test one learning objective and understand what actual budget, support and compliance would be required.”

## Script D — Investor explanation (proposed)

“We're building an accountable learning workflow rather than an undifferentiated chatbot: source review, context-specific interaction, independently distinguishable practice and portable evidence. The first narrow segment is adult engineering/computing learners where concepts can be checked with formulas, traces and clear explanations. Our main competitors are already good and often free, so the investment thesis is **not** source citations or 3D novelty. It would be a repeatable library of verified course-to-interaction templates, effective supported workflows and trust from educators and learners. Today we have code foundations and scenario economics, not validated conversion, revenue or causal learning gains. Those are the next gates before a serious scale forecast.”

## 20-second hackathon video framework (no fictitious evidence)

0–4s: **Need**: a consenting actor or synthetic scripted example encounters an actual difficult concept; do not imply genuine student data.
4–10s: **Mechanism**: corrected source quote → KAIRO explains/points to exact anchor; keep on-screen readable.
10–16s: **Proof**: user predicts one meaningful parameter change → sees correct consequence → attempts a task; use live capture only if authorised and actually exercised.
16–20s: **Value**: the saved attempt/reading fallback and memorable line “Read it. See it. Try it. Keep your working.” Mark it as a proposed storyboard until real legal-permissioned recordings exist.

**Critical**: verify the actual organiser-provided 2026 programme/portal, absolute submission cutoffs, audio/length/format, judge account/registration conditions before sending. The repository cites a 26 October 23:59 deadline and 20s video but no 2026 portal confirmation was performed here. A video compilation is not live-use proof.

## Objection-handling matrix: answer with evidence or admit unknown

| Challenge from critic | Supported answer or research needed |
|---|---|
| “Google's Gemini Notebook already cites uploaded material.” | Agree. Our proposed distinction is the connected source→domain interaction→attempt→evidence loop; benchmark it honestly on the same task. |
| “PhET is free and better at simulations.” | Agree for many supported science concepts. Use/adopt existing resources where licences permit. Prove if KAIRO's lesson-linked task adds measurable value rather than cloning PhET. |
| “How do you know students need this?” | Report actual consenting interviews/tasks only; national enrolment counts contextualise scale, not unmet need. |
| “Does the avatar help or distract?” | A/B a readable/diagram alternative and measure comprehension, accessibility, completion and cost. Remove ornamental work if it makes tasks worse. |
| “Does it improve grades or reduce dropout?” | Not demonstrated. A controlled formative comparison and later follow-up would be needed. Don't claim causality from clicks, synthetic data or one demonstration. |
| “Can employers trust the skills?” | Practice records are not professional certification. Preserve assistance, source/rubric version and independent review boundary. |
| “What if data is expensive or connectivity fails?” | Show actual device/network KB, fallback, resume and retry in authorised tests; not merely a PWA badge. |
| “Who pays and why?” | Learner, faculty/tutor programme, sponsor and enterprise buyer are distinct hypotheses; actual paid trial/letter of intent is missing. |
| “What does it cost?” | Reproduce per-completed-journey costs with official rate tier and actual usage, including voice, retry and support; see docs/canvas-cost-research-20261024. |
| “Is KAIRO endorsed by FNB/universities/NSFAS?” | No endorsement/integration is documented. Participation in a challenge does not imply sponsorship or procurement. |

## Demonstration proof versus sales polish

The most persuasive clip is **not** a cinematic rotating avatar. It is an observable “I thought X; I changed Y; now I can explain Z from my own source; my attempt is still here after I refresh” moment. Then show that another person can navigate the same thing without instructions. If this cannot be shown reliably, simplify the demo and fix the product first.

A trusted mentor should challenge this story using real-world alternative products, accurate costs, correct engineering content and the ability to defend each claim. Another critic should try to break it with source changes, offline/no audio, wrong tenant/account, OCR ambiguity and browser refresh. The team must publish the failures as well as the successes, and avoid producing impressive but unsupported proof.

## Narrative consistency audit

Before every new public deck, pitch, landing page, 3D video, demo copy or merge into a user-visible route, compare:
1. Who is the person? Are we mixing engineering student, worker, TVET beginner, shop owner and HR buyer?
2. What concrete problem is being solved *today*? Can it be demonstrated in one task?
3. Which source is authoritative? Can user inspect original/correction/version?
4. Which step performs the claimed change? Are alternatives accessible?
5. What has actually been observed by a new user versus automated/synthetic code tests?
6. Why not a faster free alternative? What does KAIRO do measurably better?
7. How much does it cost to serve and support? What happens when credits end?
8. What are the remaining failures, security limitations and unsupported languages?
9. Does every judged criterion have a real source/test/data artifact or explicit 'not yet measured'?
10. Would an unbriefed adult learner choose to come back on their own?

**Do not turn pitch copy into a plan to capture personal data or push engagement.** The research should serve learner agency, academic integrity and genuine usefulness.
