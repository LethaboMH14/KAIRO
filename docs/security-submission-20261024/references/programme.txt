Challenges
Access to higher education
Problem statement
How might technology help young South Africans find suitable study pathways, compare
funding options, complete applications and remain supported from acceptance through
graduation?
Possible focus areas
Applications, bursaries and NSFAS guidance; cost planning; student wellness; academic
persistence; connectivity and accessible learning.
Youth employment and work readiness
Problem statement
How might we shorten the distance between young people and meaningful income by
translating skills into credible profiles, practical experience, work opportunities and career
mobility?
Possible focus areas
Skills verification; entry-level matching; micro-internships; mentorship; informal experience;
transport and data barriers.
Youth entrepreneurship and small-business growth
Problem statement
How might digital tools help young founders and township or rural businesses start, trade,
get paid, manage cash flow and reach formal markets?
Possible focus areas
Business registration; digital payments; bookkeeping; procurement access; market links;
funding readiness and fraud protection.
Financial inclusion and money confidence
Problem statement
How might we make everyday money management simpler, safer and more useful for
people with irregular incomes, limited financial knowledge or limited access to formal
services?
Possible focus areas
=== PAGE 5 ===
Budgeting; saving; responsible credit; low-data tools; multilingual support; scam
awareness; accessible design.
Safer communities and gender-based violence response
Problem statement
How might technology help people find trusted support, document incidents safely, access
emergency help and strengthen prevention without placing survivors at greater risk?
Possible focus areas
Privacy-first reporting; verified services; discreet access; referral pathways; community
education; responsible data governance.
Climate resilience and essential services
Problem statement
How might communities use data and digital services to prepare for disruptions, reduce
household costs and improve access to water, energy, transport and food?
Possible focus areas
Local alerts; service reporting; resource sharing; energy and water savings; food access;
climate-smart small businesses.

Judging Criteria
# Title Description Weight
1 Product
Is the app a complete solution or does is it far from completion?
Is it polished and does it work in a way the user would expect?
Does it do what it is meant to do, and resolve the needs of the
challenges it addresses?
Is there a market need for the application?
Does it have traction?
10
2 Creativity
Is it a new application or service that has been created?
How creative is the approach?
Is it contributing towards the improvement of society or business in
some new way?
Is it commercially, socially, politically, or personally appealing, relevant
and exciting?
Is it freshly entertaining, a new approach to an old problem or a better
way to do something more effectively?
10
3 Usability
Is the app easy to use?
Is there a clarity, simplicity and intuitive feel of how the app works and
what to do next?
10
4 Sustainability
What is the potential of the application and the team of developers
behind it?
Can the app be taken to the next level?
Is there a potential path to revenue with the app?
10
5 Impact
How much impact does the application have?
What kind of problem does it solve – a big or small one?
What is the level of indispensability and importance of the app?
10
6 Presentation
How well did the team communicate their idea/app?
Were they effective in telling their story and did they convey the
importance and relevance of the mobile application to society?
10
7 Intelligence Can the app “think” for itself, forecast, and predict the user’s likely future
behaviour by analyzing patterns in data inputs? 10
